# Code of conduct

This project is a place to work on a shared tool. These are the rules for issues, pull requests, discussions and any other space attached to this repository.

## What we expect

- **Talk about the work.** Criticise ideas, wording, code and data. Do not criticise the person who brought them.
- **Bring evidence.** A claim that something works, or does not, comes with what you ran and what happened. "It failed for me, here is the output" is always welcome.
- **Assume good faith, once.** Ask before you conclude that someone meant harm or was careless.
- **Make room.** People arrive with different languages, levels of experience and ways of working. Answer the question that was asked.
- **Accept a no.** Maintainers may decline a change. They owe you a reason; they do not owe you agreement.

## What is not acceptable

- Insults, harassment, threats, or sexual language or imagery.
- Attacks on a person for who they are: origin, gender, orientation, disability, age, religion, appearance or anything similar.
- Publishing someone's private information.
- Fabricated results. A benchmark figure that was not measured, or a claim of verification for something that was not run, is treated as misconduct here, not as a mistake of style.
- Using the project's spaces to advertise unrelated products.

## What happens

A maintainer may edit or remove a comment, close a thread, or block an account, in that order of preference and without prior notice when the behaviour is serious. If you think a decision was wrong, say so once, calmly, in a new issue.

## Reporting

Report behaviour that breaks these rules with the "Report content" option that GitHub shows on every comment, or by opening an issue that says only that you want to report a conduct problem. Do not put the details in public; a maintainer will contact you.
